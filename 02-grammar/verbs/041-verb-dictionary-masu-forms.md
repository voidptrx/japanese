# Lesson 041 — Verb Dictionary Form and ます-Form

## 1. Dictionary Form (辞書形)

The dictionary form is the basic form used to look up Japanese verbs.

It can also function directly as a plain, non-past predicate.

- 読む（よむ）— to read
- 書く（かく）— to write
- 食べる（たべる）— to eat
- 見る（みる）— to see
- 来る（くる）— to come

Example:

先生は本を読む。
The teacher reads a book.

The dictionary form is not equivalent in function to the English infinitive.

## 2. Plain vs. Polite Forms

| Plain (Dictionary) | Polite (ます) |
| ------------------ | ------------- |
| 読む               | 読みます      |
| 書く               | 書きます      |
| 食べる             | 食べます      |
| 見る               | 見ます        |
| 来る               | 来ます        |

Both forms can express non-past situations, including present habits and future events.

The main difference is the level of politeness, not tense.

## 3. Three Verb Groups

### Group 1 — Godan (五段動詞 / U-verbs)

Change the final kana to its corresponding い-row kana, then add ます.

- 読む → 読みます
- 書く → 書きます
- 行く → 行きます

### Group 2 — Ichidan (一段動詞 / Ru-verbs)

Remove the final る and add ます.

- 食べる → 食べます
- 見る → 見ます

Not all verbs ending in る belong to the Ichidan group.

### Group 3 — Irregular Verbs

- する → します (to do)
- 来る（くる）→ 来ます（きます）(to come)

These transformations must be learned separately.

## 4. Verb Stems and Okurigana

The ます-form uses a verb stem followed by ます.

**Example:**

読む → 読み + ます → 読みます

- 読: kanji
- む / みます: okurigana in the respective forms
- 読み: the ます-stem

Kanji generally preserve the lexical identity while okurigana reflect grammatical changes.

## 5. Example Sentences

先生は学校で本を読む。= The teacher reads a book at school. (Plain)

この人は本を書きます。= This person writes books. (Polite)

日本人は学校に来ます。= The Japanese person comes to school. (Polite)

## 6. Important Concepts

- The dictionary form can be a complete predicate.
- The ます-form expresses politeness, not future tense.
- Godan, Ichidan, and irregular verbs transform differently.
- Learn each verb together with its dictionary form and group.
- Practice kanji and okurigana through actual vocabulary.
