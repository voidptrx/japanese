# Lesson 050 — Verb て-Form Formation (～て)

## 1. Overview

The ～て form connects verbs to other actions and constructions.

It does not independently express tense or politeness.

This lesson covers affirmative verb て-forms and basic action sequences.

## 2. Relationship to the ～た Form

The ～て form follows the same conjugation patterns as the plain affirmative past form.

- ～た → ～て
- ～だ → ～で

**Examples:**

- 食べた → 食べて
- 読んだ → 読んで
- 書いた → 書いて

The ～て form itself is not a past-tense form.

## 3. Godan Verbs

| Dictionary Ending | て-Form Ending | Example       |
| ----------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| う・つ・る        | って           | 買う → 買って |
| む・ぶ・ぬ        | んで           | 読む → 読んで |
| く                | いて           | 書く → 書いて |
| ぐ                | いで           | 泳ぐ → 泳いで |
| す                | して           | 話す → 話して |

Special exception:

- 行く → 行って (not 行いて)

## 4. Ichidan Verbs

Remove the final る and add て.

- 食べる → 食べて
- 見る → 見て
- 起きる → 起きて

## 5. Irregular Verbs

- する → して
- 来る（くる）→ 来て（きて）

## 6. Connecting Actions

The ～て form can connect actions in a sequence.

In a basic action sequence, the final predicate determines tense and politeness.

- 学校に行って、本を読んだ。
  = I went to school and read a book.

- 学校に行って、本を読みます。
  = I go to school and read a book.

- 先生が来て、学生が本を読みました。
  = The teacher came, and the student read a book.

Connected actions can have different subjects.

## 7. Important Concepts

- The ～て form is not inherently past or present.
- Godan verbs follow ending-dependent transformations.
- Ichidan verbs replace る with て.
- する and 来る are irregular.
- 行く → 行って is a special exception.
- The final predicate determines tense and politeness in basic action sequences.
- Other constructions, including ～てください and ～ています, will be studied separately.
