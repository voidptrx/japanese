## 2. What is the Subject Particle が?

The particle `が` marks the grammatical subject of a clause.

Example:

さくらがきます。= Sakura will come.

- さくら = Sakura
- が = subject marker
- きます = comes / will come

The subject is the person or thing connected to the predicate.

`が` is pronounced "ga".

## 3. Cure Dolly's Train Model

Cure Dolly explains the basic Japanese sentence using a train analogy.

The main carriage (A) represents the subject. The engine (B) represents the predicate. The particle `が` links the subject to the predicate.

Example:

さくらがきます。\
A: さくら\
が: subject marker\
B: きます

This model is useful for understanding the subject-predicate relationship.

Cure Dolly also describes an "invisible carriage" when the subject is not explicitly stated.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - Japanese frequently omits information understood from context.
> - An omitted subject does not mean that the subject-predicate relationship disappears.
> - The invisible `が` is part of Cure Dolly's explanatory model, not a particle that must literally be inserted into every sentence.

## 4. Topic は vs. Subject が

`は` marks the topic. `が` marks the grammatical subject.

Compare:

ねこはかわいいです。= As for cats, the are cute.\
ねこがかわいいです。= The cat is cute.

In the first sentence, ねこ is presented as the topic. In the second sentence, ねこ is explicitly marked as the subject of かわいいです.\
Both sentences can have similar translation. The important distinction is how the information is presented.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - A topic can also be the grammatical subject.
> - A sentence using `は` can still have a subject.
> - `が` does not always imply strong emphasis.
> - `は` does not always indicate old information.
> - `が` does not always indicate new information.
> - Context determines the appropriate interpretation.

## 5. は and が in Context

Consider the question:

だれがきますか。= Who will come?

A natural answer is:\
さくらがきます。= Sakura will come.

Here, `が` marks the person being identified.

Now consider a conversation already about Sakura:

さくらはきます。= As for Sakura, she will come.

Here, Sakura is presented as the topic.

Both sentences are grammatically possible. The choice depends on what the conversation is about and what information is being supplied.

## 6. Topic and Subject in the Same Sentence

`は` and `が` can appear together because they have different functions.

Example:

きょうはさくらがきます。= Sakura will come today.

- きょうは = today (topic)
- さくらが = Sakura (subject)
- きます = will come (predicate)

The topic provides the conversational frame. The subject identifies the person or thing connected to the predicate.

Another example:

きょうはあめがふります。= It will rain today.

- きょうは = today (topic)
- あめが = rain (subject)
- ふります = rains / will rain (predicate)

## 7. が with Adjectives

`が` can mark the subject of an adjective predicate.

Example:

ねこがかわいいです。= The cat is cute.

- ねこが = subject
- かわいいです = adjective predicate

Another example:

さくらはげんきです。= Sakura is well.

げんき is a な-adjective.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - げんきです is correct.
> - げんきます is incorrect.
> - ます is used with verb forms, not directly with adjective predicates.

## 8. が with Existence Verbs

Japanese uses two important existence verbs.

### います

Used primarily for people and animals.

ねこがいます。= There is a cat.

### あります

Used primarily for inanimate things.

ほんがあります。= There is a book.

In both examples, `が` marks the thing whose existence is being stated.

Compare:

ねこがいます。= There is a cat.\
ねこはいます。= As for the cat, it is here / it exists.

The second sentence presents the cat as the topic and requires an appropriate context.

## 9. が Is Not Only for Answering Questions

`が` is often used to identify the answer to a question such as:

だれがきますか。= Who will come?

However, `が` is not limited to this function.

It can also appear when announcing an event.

Example:

あめがふっています。= It is raining.

No previous question is required.

`が` marks the subject of the predicate.

## 10. Important Concepts

- `が` marks the grammatical subject.
- `は` marks the topic.
- Topic and subject are different concepts.
- The topic can also be the subject.
- `は` and `が` can appear in the same sentence.
- `が` can be used with verbs, adjectives and existence expressions.
- `が` does not always mean "new information".
- `は` does not always mean "old information".
- A missing subject may be understood from context.
- Cure Dolly's train model helps illustrate the subject-predicate relationship.
- Context is essential when choosing `は` or `が`.
