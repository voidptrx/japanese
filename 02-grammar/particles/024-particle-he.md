# Lesson 024 - Particle へ

## 1. What I learned

- I learned the pronunciation of the particle `へ`.
- I can use `へ` to indicate the direction of movement.
- I understand the basic difference between directional `へ` and destination `に`.
- I can attach `へ` to a complete noun phrase.
- I can combine `へ` with particles learned in previous lessons.
- I understand that `へ` does not replace every use of `に`.

## 2. What is the Particle へ?

In the use studied in this lesson, `へ` marks the direction of movement.

Place + `へ` + movement verb

Example:

がっこうへいきます。= I go to school. / I go toward school.

がっこうへ identifies the direction of movement.

## 3. Cure Dolly's Explanation

Cure Dolly introduces this use in Chapter 8, "The に and へ Particles",
in the section "The へ Particle".

The basic comparison is with `に` marking the destination of movement.

With familiar movement verbs, `へ` can often be used with the same place,
presenting it as the direction of movement.

Chapter 8b, "Particles explained.", also discusses this directional role.
This lesson focuses on that basic use.

## 4. Pronunciation and Spelling

The particle `へ` is pronounced `え` or "e". It is still written `へ`.

Compare:

- へや: the first kana is pronounced "he".
- へやへ: the final `へ` is a particle and is pronounced "e".

The role of the kana determines its pronunciation here.

## 5. へ with Movement Verbs

Example:

がっこうへいきます。= I go to school.\
うちへかえります。= I return home.

In each example, `へ` marks the direction of movement.

The subject is omitted. "I" is supplied by the exercise context.

## 6. に and へ

| Sentence             | Basic focus                         |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| がっこうにいきます。 | school as the destination           |
| がっこうへいきます。 | school as the direction of movement |

Both sentences can be translated as "I go to school".

With common movement verbs such as いきます and かえります,
the two particles often overlap in use.

うちにかえります。= I return home.\
うちへかえります。= I return home. / I return toward home.

Using `へ` does not imply that the person will fail to reach the destination.

Whether arrival actually occurs is understood from the whole sentence and its context.

## 7. へ with Noun Phrases

Example:

[せんせいのへや] へいきます。= I go to the teacher's room.

The complete place phrase is せんせいのへや.

`の` expresses a relationship inside the noun phrase.
`へ` follows the complete phrase and marks the direction of movement.

The head noun inside the phrase is へや.

The brackets are analysis aids. They are not required Japanese punctuation.

## 8. へ with と and で

Example:

せんせいとでんしゃでがっこうへいきます。= I go to school by train with the teacher.

| Part       | Role                                 |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------ |
| せんせいと | companion: with the teacher          |
| でんしゃで | transportation: by train             |
| がっこうへ | direction of movement: toward school |
| いきます   | predicate: go                        |

Each particle keeps its own role.

## 9. へ with も

Example:

わたしもせんせいのへやへいきます。= I also go to the teacher's room.

`も` adds わたし as another person to whom the statement applies.
`へ` marks the complete phrase せんせいのへや as the direction of movement.
The person added by `も` is わたし.

## 10. Sentence Analysis

Example:

せんせいのねことわたしのねこがこうえんへいきます。=
The teacher's cat and my cat go to the park.

| Part                           | Role                                              |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| せんせいのねことわたしのねこが | coordinated subject: the teacher's cat and my cat |
| こうえんへ                     | direction of movement: toward the park            |
| いきます                       | predicate: go                                     |

The complete subject phrase is:

［せんせいのねこ］と［わたしのねこ］

`の` expresses relationships inside the two noun phrases.
`と` joins the phrases.
`が` marks the complete coordinated group as the subject.
`へ` marks the direction of the cats' movement.

## 11. Important Distinctions

### Direction and location of existence

Example:

ねこがへやにいます。= The cat is in the room.

`に` marks the location where the cat is present. For this meaning, use `に`.

Example:

ねこがへやへいきます。= The cat goes to the room.

`へ` marks the direction of movement.

### Direction and location of an action

Example:

へやでほんをよみます。= I read a book in the room.

`で` marks the location of the reading action.

Example:

へやへいきます。= I go to the room.

`へ` marks the direction of movement.

### Time に and directional へ

はちじにともだちとうちへかえります。= At eight o'clock, I return home with my friend.

- はちじに: time, at eight o'clock
- ともだちと: companion, with my friend
- うちへ: direction of movement, toward home
- かえります: predicate, return

For this time expression, use `に`.

`へ` does not replace `に` in the time and existence patterns studied here.

## 12. Important Concepts

- The particle `へ` is pronounced え.
- In this lesson, `へ` marks the direction of movement.
- `に` can mark the destination of movement.
- With familiar movement verbs, `に` and `へ` often overlap in use.
- Using `へ` does not imply failure to arrive.
- `へ` can follow a complete noun phrase.
- Other particles retain their own roles.
- `へ` is not a general replacement for `に`.
- Other uses of `へ` will be studied later.
