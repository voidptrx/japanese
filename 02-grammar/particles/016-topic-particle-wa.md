# Lesson 016 - Topic Particle は

## 1. What I learned

- I learned that `は` marks the topic of a Japanese sentence.
- I understand the basic topic-comment structure.
- I learned that the topic is not necessarily the grammatical subject.
- I understand that the topic can be a word or a larger phrase.
- I learned that a known topic does not always need to be repeated.
- I learned that `は` can express contrast depending on context.
- I can use `は` in simple Japanese sentences.

## 2. What is the Topic Particle は?

The particle `は` marks the topic of a sentence.
The topic tells us what the sentence is about.

Example:

わたしはせんせいです。= I am a teacher.

- わたし = I
- は = topic marker
- せんせいです = am a teacher

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - The topic particle is written `は`.
> - As a particle, `は` is pronounced `wa`.
> - It is not written わ.

## 3. Topic-Comment Structure

A useful way to understand Japanese sentences is:

Topic + `は` + Comment

The comment provides information about the topic.

Example:

このほんはおもしろいです。= This book is interesting.

- このほん = this book (topic)
- は = topic marker
- おもしろいです = is interesting (comment)

The topic can be an entire phrase, not just one word.

The topic-comment model helps explain how information is organized in Japanese sentences.

## 4. Topic vs. Subject

The topic and the grammatical subject are not necessarily the same.

Example:

ねこはかわいいです。= The cat is cute.

Here, ねこ is the topic and can also be understood as the grammatical subject.

Compare:

きょうはさむいです。= It is cold today.

Here, きょう (today) is the topic and a time expression.

The particle `は` does not automatically identify the grammatical subject.

## 5. Omitted Topics

Japanese does not require an explicit topic in every sentence.

Compare:

わたしはみずをのみます。= I drink water.
みずをのみます。= Someone drinks water, depending on context.

In the second sentence, the person drinking is not explicitly identified.

The context may make this information clear.

Example dialogue:

A: このほんはおもしろいですか。\
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Is this book interesting?

B: はい、おもしろいです。\
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Yes, it is interesting.

The second speaker does not need to repeat このほんは because the topic is already understood.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - A sentence can be complete without an explicit `は`.
> - A known topic does not always need to be repeated.
> - Do not automatically assume an omitted topic is わたし.

## 6. Contrastive は

Depending on context, `は` can also express contrast.

Example:

パンはたべます。= As for bread, I eat it.

In an appropriate context, this may suggest a contrast with other foods.

Compare:

パンをたべます。= I eat bread.
パンはたべます。= As for bread, I eat it.

In the second sentence, パン is presented as the topic.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - `は` does not always express contrast.
> - Contrast depends on context.
> - The basic function of `は` is topic marking.

## 7. は and を

The particle `を` marks the direct object.

The particle `は` marks the topic.

Compare:

みずをのみます。= I drink water / someone drinks water.
みずはのみます。= As for water, someone drinks it.

In the second sentence, water is presented as the topic, possibly in contrast with other drinks.

When a direct object is topicalized with `は`, `は` normally replaces `を`.

## 8. Important Concepts

- `は` marks the topic of a sentence.
- The topic tells us what the comment is about.
- The topic and grammatical subject are different concepts.
- The topic can be a word or a larger phrase.
- The topic may be omitted when understood from context.
- `は` can express contrast, but contrast is not mandatory.
- When an object becomes the topic, `は` normally replaces `を`.
- The topic particle `は` is pronounced "wa".
- Understanding context is essential for natural Japanese.
