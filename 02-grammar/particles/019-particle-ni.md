# Lesson 019 - Particle に

## 1. What I learned

- I learned that `に` has several grammatical functions.
- I learned how `に` marks the destination of movement.
- I learned how `に` marks the location of existence.
- I learned how `に` marks specific points in time.
- I learned how `に` marks the recipient of an action.
- I understand the basic distinction between `に` and `で`.
- I can use `に` with `は`, `が` and `を` in simple sentences.

## 2. The Basic Idea of に

The particle `に` can mark a destination, location, specific time or recipient, depending on the
predicate and context.

Cure Dolly uses the idea of a "target" to connect several uses of `に`.

This is a useful explanatory model, but it should not be treated as a universal rule.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - `に` does not have one fixed English or Turkish translation.
> - Its function depends on the sentence.
> - Not every `に` marks a physical destination.

## 3. に - Destination of Movement

`に` can mark the destination of a movement.

Example:

さくらがこうえんにいきます。= Sakura goes / will go to the park.

- さくらが = subject
- こうえんに = destination
- いきます = predicate

Example:

がっこうにいきます。= Go / will go to school, depending on context.\
うちにかえります。= Return / will return home, depending on context.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> こうえんにいきます。- Correct\
> こうえんをいきます。- Incorrect for the intended meaning "go to the park".
>
> The destination is not the direct object of いきます.

## 4. に - Location of Existence

`に` marks the location where someone or something exists, especially with います and あります.

Example:

へやにねこがいます。= There is a cat in the room.

- へやに = location of existence
- ねこが = subject
- います = existence predicate

Example:

へやにほんがあります。= There is a book in the room.

- へやに = location
- ほんが = subject
- あります = predicate

The location is not automatically the subject.

Another possible word order:\
ほんがへやにあります。= The book is in the room.

The grammatical roles are preserved by the particles.
Word order can affect information structure and naturalness.

## 5. に - Specific Points in Time

`に` can mark a specific point in time.

Example:

はちじにおきます。= I get up / will get up at eight o'clock, depending on context.

- はちじに = specific time
- おきます = predicate

Specific clock times generally use `に`. However, not every time expression takes `に`.

Compare:

はちじにおきます。= Get up at eight o'clock.\
あしたいきます。= Go / will go tomorrow.\
きょうはさむいです。= It is cold today.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - Specific clock times generally take `に`.
> - Relative time expressions such as `きょう` and `あした` normally do not take temporal `に`.

## 6. に - Recipient of an Action

`に` can mark the recipient of something being given.

Example:

わたしはさくらにほんをあげます。= I give / will give Sakura a book.

- わたしは = topic
- さくら = recipient
- ほんを = direct object
- あげます = predicate

Compare:

さくらがほんをよみます。= Sakura reads / will read a book.\
さくらにほんをあげます。= Someone gives / will give Sakura a book, depending on context.

In the first sentence, Sakura is the subject. In the second sentence, Sakura is the recipient.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - `に` marks the recipient in the giving example.
> - `を` marks the thing being given.
> - The recipient is not the direct object.

## 7. Different Functions of に

Compare:

こうえんにいきます。= Go to the park. (Destination)\
こうえんにねこがいます。= There is a cat in the park. (Location of existence)\
はちじにおきます。= Get up at eight o'clock. (Specific time)\
さくらにほんをあげます。= Give Sakura a book. (Recipient)

The same particle performs different functions.
Its meaning must be understood through the entire sentence.

## 8. に vs. で - Basic Location Difference

At the beginner level, an important distinction is:

`に` can mark the location of existence.
`で` can mark the place where an action occurs.

Compare:

へやにねこがいます。= There is a cat in the room.\
へやでほんをよみます。= Someone reads a book in the room.\
へやにいきます。= Someone goes to the room.

These three sentences express different relations:

- へやに + います: location of existence
- へやで + よみます: place of an action
- へやに + いきます: destination of movement

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - `に` and `で` are not interchangeable.
> - This is a beginner-level distinction, not a complete account of either particle.
> - The particle `で` will receive its own lesson.

## 9. Multiple に Particles in One Sentence

A sentence can contain more than one `に`.

Example:

はちじにさくらにほんをあげます。= I will give Sakura a book at eight o'clock.

- はちじに = specific time
- さくらに = recipient
- ほんを = direct object
- あげます = predicate

The two `に` particles have different functions.
The subject is not explicitly stated.
Context determines who gives the book.

## 10. に with Other Particles

Example:

わたしはさくらにみずをあげます。= I give / will give Sakura water.

- わたしは = topic
- さくらに = recipient
- みずを = direct object
- あげます = predicate

Example:

さくらがこうえんでほんをよみます。= Sakura reads / will read a book in the park.

- さくらが = subject
- こうえんで = place of the action
- ほんを = direct object
- よみます = predicate

Particles identify different relationships within the sentence.

## 11. Important Concepts

- `に` has several functions.
- `に` can mark a movement destination.
- `に` can mark the location of existence.
- `に` can mark a specific time point.
- `に` can mark the recipient of an action.
- `で` commonly marks where an action occurs.
- The function of `に` depends on the predicate and context.
- A destination is not automatically a direct object.
- A location is not automatically the subject.
- More than one `に` can appear in the same sentence.
- Particle choice and word order both contribute to Japanese sentence structure.
