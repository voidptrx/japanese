# Lesson 023 - Particle と

## 1. What I learned

- I learned how `と` joins nouns and noun phrases.
- I can identify a coordinated subject or object.
- I can distinguish `と` from `の`.
- I can use `と` to mark a companion.
- I can distinguish a companion marked by `と` from transportation marked by `で`.
- I can combine these patterns with particles learned in previous lessons.

## 2. What is the Particle と?

This lesson covers two basic uses of `と`:

- Joining nouns: `A と B` = A and B
- Marking a companion: person `と` = with that person.

The sentence structure and context help us identify the use.

## 3. Cure Dolly's Explanation

Cure Dolly discusses noun-listing `と` in Chapter 16, in the section
"The や particle + exclusive and".

The structural point is that nouns can form one coordinated group.

The book calls this use "exclusive and". In this lesson, we understand it as listing the named
items together. The relevant scope of the list depends on context.

Quotation `と`, discussed in Chapter 12, will be studied separately.

## 4. と - Joining Nouns

Noun A + `と` + Noun B

Example:

りんごとパン = apples and bread

Both nouns are members of the group.

`と` can also join complete noun phrases:

せんせいのほんとわたしのほん = the teacher's book and my book

## 5. と - A Coordinated Object

Example:

[りんごとパン] をたべます。= I eat apples and bread.

The complete object phrase is りんごとパン.

`と` joins the two nouns. `を` marks the complete group as the object of たべます.

Example:

[みずとコーヒー] をのみます。= I drink water and coffee.

The complete object phrase is みずとコーヒー.

## 6. と - A Coordinated Subject

Example:

「せんせいとねこ」がへやにいます。= The teacher and the cat are in the room.

The complete subject phrase is せんせいとねこ.

`と` joins two nouns. `が` marks the complete group as the subject.
`に` marks the location where the teacher and the cat are present.

The brackets are analysis aids. They are not required Japanese punctuation.

## 7. と - A Companion

Example:

ともだちとこうえんにいきます。= I go to the park with my friend.

ともだちと identifies the companion. こうえんに identifies the destination.

In this sentence, `と` means "with", rather than joining two explicitly listed nouns.

The subject is omitted. "I" is supplied by the exercise context.

## 8. と and の

| Phrase         | Meaning                 | Structure                          |
| -------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| せんせいのねこ | the teacher's cat       | せんせい modifies ねこ             |
| せんせいとねこ | the teacher and the cat | two members of a coordinated group |
| わたしのほん   | my book                 | わたし modifies ほん               |
| わたしとほん   | me and a book           | two members of a coordinated group |

For the meaning "the teacher's book and my book":

[せんせいのほん] と [わたしのほん]

`の` expresses a relationship inside each noun phrase.
`と` joins the two complete noun phrases.

## 9. Companionship and Transportation

Example:

わたしはともだちとバスでがっこうにいきます。= I go to school by bus with my friend.

| Part       | Role                      |
| ---------- | ------------------------- |
| わたしは   | topic: I                  |
| ともだちと | companion: with my friend |
| バスで     | transportation: by bus    |
| がっこうに | destination: to school    |
| いきます   | predicate: go             |

`と` identifies the companion.
`で` identifies the means of transportation.

## 10. Important Distinction

### と and も

Example:

わたしもせんせいとがっこうにいきます。= I also go to school with the teacher.

- `も` adds わたし as another person to whom the statement applies.
- `と` marks せんせい as the companion.
- `に` marks がっこう as the destination.

The person added by `も` is わたし.

### Destination and action location

Example:

こうえんにいきます。= I go to the park.

`に` marks the destination.

Example:

こうえんでほんをよみます。= I read a book in the park.

`で` marks the location of the reading action.

### Noun groups and action sequences

The `A と B` pattern studied here joins nouns or noun phrases.

Ways to connect predicates, such as "eat and drink", will be studied separately.

## 12. Important Concepts

- `と` can join nouns or complete noun phrases.
- A particle after a coordinated group can mark the role of the whole group.
- `の` expresses a relationship inside a noun phrase.
- `と` can identify a companion.
- `で` can identify transportation or an instrument.
- The role in the sentence determines the particle.
- The subject can be omitted when understood from context.
- This lesson covers noun coordination and companionship. Other uses of `と` will be studied later.
