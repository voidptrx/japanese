# Lesson 022 - Particle も

## 1. What I learned

- I learned the basic additive meaning of `も`.
- I can distinguish adding a person from adding an object.
- I understand that the position of `も` determines which elements is included.
- I can use `にも` for an additional destination.
- I can use `でも` for an additional location of an action.
- I can attach `も` to a complete noun phrase.

## 2. What is the Particle も?

In its basic additive use, `も` means "also", "too" or "as well".
It follows the element being included.
The relevant comparison may be stated explicitly or understood from context.

## 3. Cure Dolly's Explanation

Cure Dolly introduces this use in Chapter 14, "Adverbs and the `も` Particle",
in the section "The `も` particle".

The starting idea is that information applying to one item also applies to another.
This is a useful structural comparison.
It should not be treated as a universal description of every use of `も`.

In this lesson, we apply the basic additive meaning to people, objects and location phrases.

## 4. も - Adding a Person

Example:

せんせいはみずをのみます。= The teacher drinks water.\
わたしもみずをのみます。= I drink water **too**.

The person being added is わたし.

The statement about drinking water applies to the teacher and to me.

## 5. も - Adding an Object

Example:

わたしはみずをのみます。= I drink water.\
コーヒーものみます。= I drink coffee **too**.

The thing being added is コーヒー.

In this example, the action remains drinking. Coffee is added to the things I drink.

## 6. Position and Focus

| Japanese                     | Meaning                                            | Element being added |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| わたしもコーヒーをのみます。 | I drink coffee too.                                | person: I           |
| わたしはコーヒーものみます。 | I also drink coffee, in addition to another drink. | object: coffee      |

The English translations can sound similar, but the Japanese sentences
place `も` after different elements.

In the basic patterns studied here:

| Basic phrase | Additive form |
| ------------ | ------------- |
| わたしは     | わたしも      |
| ねこが       | ねこも        |
| ほんを       | ほんも        |
| がっこうに   | がっこうにも  |
| がっこうで   | がっこうでも  |

`も` replaces `は`, `が` or `を` in these basic patterns.

`に` and `で` remain before `も` because they retain their roles.

## 7. も with に - An Additional Destination

Example:

こうえんにいきます。= I go to the park.\
がっこうにもいきます。= I go to school **too**.

- `に` marks the destination.
- `も` includes school as an additional destination.

The order is `に + も`.

## 8. も with で - An Additional Action Location

Example:

へやでほんをよみます。= I read a book in the room.\
がっこうでもほんをよみます。= I read a book at school **too**.

- `で` marks the location of the action.
- `も` includes school as an additional location.

The order is `で + も`.

## 9. も with Noun Phrases

Example:

せんせいのねこもへやにいます。= The teacher's cat is in the room too.

も follows the complete phrase せんせいのねこ.

Example:

せんせいのほんもよみます。= I read the teacher's book too.

も follows the complete object phrase せんせいのほん.

Example:

せんせいのねこのなまえもかきます。= I write the name of the teacher's cat too.

The complete object phrase is せんせいのねこのなまえ.
Its head noun is なまえ.

`の` expresses relationships inside the noun phrase.
`も` applies to the complete phrase.

## 10. Sentence Analysis

Example:

がっこうでもわたしのなまえをペンでかきます。= At school too, I write my name with a pen.

| Part             | Role                                                          |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| がっこうでも     | additional location: で marks the action location; も adds it |
| わたしのなまえを | object: my name                                               |
| ペンで           | instrument: with a pen                                        |
| かきます         | predicate: write                                              |

The first `で` marks a location. The second `で` marks an instrument.

`も` follows the first `で`, so school is the location being added.

The subject is omitted in Japanese. “I” is supplied by the exercise context.

## 11. Important Distinctions

### は and も

For the intended meaning “The teacher drinks water too”:

- Incorrect: せんせいはもみずをのみます。
- Correct: せんせいもみずをのみます。

In this basic construction, `も` takes the place of `は`.

### Adding a person and adding an object

Example:

わたしもほんをよみます。= I read books too.

The person being added is わたし.

Example:

わたしはほんもよみます。= I read books as well as other things.

The object being added is ほん.

In the second example, reading remains the action.
Books are added to the things read.

### にも and でも

Example:

こうえんにもいきます: I go to the park too.\
こうえんでもほんをよみます: I read a book in the park too.

に retains its destination role.
で retains its action-location role.

## 12. Important Concepts

- Basic additive `も` means "also", "too" or "as well".
- The position of `も` determines which element is included.
- `も` can apply to a complete noun phrase.
- The grammatical role of that phrase is understood from the sentence structure and context.
- In the basic patterns studied here, `も` replaces `は`, `が`, or `を`.
- `に` and `で` remain before `も`.
- The relevant comparison can be understood from context.
- Other uses of `も` will be studied separately.
