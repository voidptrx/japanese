# Lesson 030 - Demonstratives: これ, それ, あれ

## 1. What I learned

- I learned how to distinguish **これ**, **それ** and **あれ**.
- I understand their relationship to the speaker and listener.
- I can use these demonstratives as noun-like expression.
- I can combine them with familiar particles such as **は**, **が**, **も** and **を**.
- I can form statements and polite questions using demonstratives.
- I understand the basic distinction between **これ** and **この**.

## 2. What are Demonstratives?

Demonstratives are expressions used to refer to particular people, things or places.

Japanese uses a systematic group of demonstratives based on the **こ-そ-あ-ど** pattern.

This lesson focuses on three demonstratives that refer to things:

| Demonstrative | Meaning         | Basic reference   |
| ------------- | --------------- | ----------------- |
| これ          | this / this one | Near the speaker  |
| それ          | that / that one | Near the listener |
| あれ          | that over there | Away from both    |

The basic distinction depends on the relationship between the object, the speaker and the listener.

- **これ** refers to something near the speaker.
- **それ** refers to something near the listener.
- **あれ** refers to something away from both the speaker and the listener.

<u>Example:</u>

A speaker is holding a book.

これはほんです。= This is a book.

The listener is holding a book.

それはほんです。= That is a book.

A book is located away from both people.

あれはほんです。= That over there is a book.

The grammatical structure remains the same.

Only the demonstrative changes according to the object being identified.

## 3. Cure Dolly's Explanation

Cure Dolly discusses demonstratives in Chapter 20, "Directionals: それ-その-そんな-そう etc."

She explains the **こ-そ-あ-ど** system as a family of expressions that can refer to physical locations and extend to more abstract relationships.

The basic pattern is:

- **こ**: associated with the speaker or something close to the speaker.
- **そ**: associated with the listener or something close to the listener.
- **あ**: associated with something away from both.
- **ど**: used to form related question words.

Cure Dolly describes **これ**, **それ**, **あれ** and **どれ** as the れ-group.

These expressions refer to things or entities.

In her explanation, the れ-group can be understood as referring to a thing or being rather than merely describing a following noun.

For example:

**これ** = this thing

**それ** = that thing

**あれ** = that thing over there

This is important because these demonstratives can function independently as noun-like expressions.

They do not require another noun to complete their meaning.

The related question word **どれ** will be studied separately.

## 4. Demonstratives as Noun-Like Expressions

**これ**, **それ** and **あれ** can function like nouns in Japanese sentences.

This means they can appear with familiar grammatical particles.

<u>Example:</u>

これはほんです。= This is a book.

Analyze the sentence as:

[これ] は [ほんです]。

| Component      | Expression |
| -------------- | ---------- |
| Topic          | これ       |
| Topic particle | は         |
| Predicate      | ほんです   |

The demonstrative **これ** identifies the object being discussed.

The particle **は** presents that object as the topic.

The predicate **ほんです** identifies the object as a book.

Another example:

それはせんせいのほんです。= That is the teacher's book.

| Component      | Expression         |
| -------------- | ------------------ |
| Topic          | それ               |
| Topic particle | は                 |
| Predicate      | せんせいのほんです |

Here, **それ** refers to an object associated with the listener.

The predicate identifies the object as the teacher's book.

The demonstrative does not change the structure of the noun sentence.

## 5. Demonstratives with Familiar Particles

Because demonstratives function as noun-like expressions, they can be combines with particles already studied.

Compare:

| Expression | Function                        |
| ---------- | ------------------------------- |
| これは     | Presents this as the topic      |
| それが     | Marks that as the subject       |
| あれも     | Adds that one as well           |
| これを     | Marks this as the direct object |

### Demonstratives with は

<u>Example:</u>

これはわたしのほんです。= This is my book.

**これ** identifies the object.

**は** presents the object as the topic.

### Demonstratives with が

<u>Example:</u>

それがわたしのほんです。= That is my book.

Here, **が** marks **それ** as the grammatical subject.

The sentence can identify that particular object as the speaker's book.

The choice between **は** and **が** depends on the information being presented and the conversational context.

### Demonstratives with も

<u>Example:</u>

あれもわたしのほんです。= That over there is also my book.

Here, **も** adds **あれ** as another object to which the statement applies.

### Demonstratives with を

<u>Example:</u>

せんせいはそれをよみます。= The teacher reads that.

Here, **それ** is the direct object of **よみます**.

The particle **を** marks the object being read.

The familiar particles keep their grammatical roles when used with demonstratives.

## 6. は vs. も with Demonstratives

Compare:

これはせんせいのほんです。= This is the teacher's book.

これもせんせいのほんです。= This is also the teacher's book.

Both sentences identify the object as the teacher's book.

However, they present the information differently.

In the first sentence:

- **これ** identifies the object.
- **は** marks the object as the topic.
- The sentence states that this object is the teacher's book.

In the second sentence:

- **これ** identifies the object.
- **も** adds an additive meaning.
- The sentence indicates that this object is also the teacher's book.

The particle **も** adds the object to another relevant object or situation established by context.

In this basic pattern, **も** appears where **は** could otherwise mark the topic.

<u>Example:</u>

それもせんせいのほんですか。= Is that also the teacher's book?

Here, **も** adds the object identified by **それ**.

The question particle **か** turns the statement into a polite question.

The additive meaning of **も** remains present in the question.

## 7. Demonstratives in Polite Questions

Demonstratives can be used with the familiar question patterns studied in Lesson 029.

Basic noun sentence pattern:

**AはBですか。**

<u>Example:</u>

それはせんせいのほんですか。= Is that the teacher's book?

Analyze the sentence as:

[それ] は [せんせいのほんです] か。

| Component         | Expression         |
| ----------------- | ------------------ |
| Topic             | それ               |
| Predicate         | せんせいのほんです |
| Question particle | か                 |

The demonstrative **それ** identifies the object being discussed.

The predicate **せんせいのほんです** identifies the object as the teacher's book.

The particle **か** marks the polite question.

Another example:

これもほんですか。= Is this also a book?

Here, **も** preserves the additive meaning while **か** marks the question.

Demonstratives can also appear as objects in questions with familiar verb forms.

<u>Example:</u>

せんせいはそれをよみますか。= Does the teacher read that?

The sentence contains a topic, an object, a predicate and a question particle.

The familiar grammatical structure remains unchanged.

## 8. Demonstratives and の

Demonstratives can appear in sentences containing noun phrases connected by **の**.

<u>Example:</u>

あれはわたしのともだちのほんです。=
That over there is my friend's book.

The demonstrative **あれ** identifies the object being discussed.

The predicate contains a noun phrase with two **の** particles.

Analyze the noun phrase as:

［わたしのともだち］のほん

The relationships are:

**わたしのともだち** = my friend

**わたしのともだちのほん** = my friend's book

The first **の** connects わたし and ともだち.

The second **の** connects the complete noun phrase わたしのともだち with ほん.

The head noun of the complete predicate noun phrase is **ほん**.

The demonstrative **あれ** does not change these relationships.

It independently identifies the object being described.

## 9. The Difference Between これ and この

Cure Dolly and Tae Kim both distinguish between demonstratives that stand independently and demonstrative expressions that modify a following noun.

Compare:

| Expression | Meaning   | Function                    |
| ---------- | --------- | --------------------------- |
| これ       | this      | Stands independently        |
| このほん   | this book | Modifies the following noun |

**これ** refers to an object independently.

**この** must be followed by a noun.

<u>Example:</u>

これはほんです。= This is a book.

Here, **これ** functions independently.

Compare:

このほん = this book

Here, **この** identifies which book is being referred to.

The distinction is grammatical.

Incorrect:

これほん

Correct:

このほん

**これほん** cannot be used to mean "this book."

The **この・その・あの** group will be studied in the next lesson.

## 10. Sentence Analysis

<u>Example:</u>

せんせいはそれをよみますか。=
Does the teacher read that?

| Part       | Role               |
| ---------- | ------------------ |
| せんせいは | topic: the teacher |
| それを     | object: that       |
| よみます   | predicate: read    |
| か         | question particle  |

The demonstrative **それ** refers to the object being read.

The particle **を** marks that object.

The familiar non-past verb form can describe habitual actions or future actions, depending on context.

Another example:

それもせんせいのほんですか。=
Is that also the teacher's book?

| Part               | Role                                |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| それも             | demonstrative with additive meaning |
| せんせいのほんです | predicate                           |
| か                 | question particle                   |

The particle **も** adds **それ** to the relevant context.

The question particle **か** follows the complete predicate.

Another example:

あれはわたしのともだちのほんです。=
That over there is my friend's book.

| Part                       | Role                   |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| あれは                     | topic: that over there |
| わたしのともだちのほんです | predicate              |

The predicate contains a complete noun phrase.

Its head noun is **ほん**.

The two **の** particles express relationships within that noun phrase.

## 11. Important Distinctions

### Physical distance and context

The basic physical meanings are:

- **これ**: near the speaker
- **それ**: near the listener
- **あれ**: away from both

However, demonstratives can also refer to things established through conversation.

Their interpretation depends on context.

The physical-distance explanation is the foundation studied in this lesson.

### Demonstratives and grammatical particles

Demonstratives function as noun-like expressions.

They can be followed by familiar particles.

The particle determines the grammatical relationship or discourse function.

For example:

- **これは**: topic
- **それが**: subject
- **あれも**: additive meaning
- **これを**: direct object

The demonstrative identifies the thing.

The particle indicates how that expression functions in the sentence.

### Demonstratives and noun modification

**これ** and **この** are not interchangeable.

- **これ** can stand independently.
- **この** must modify a following noun.

Do not place **これ** directly before a noun to express "this noun."

### Demonstratives and questions

The question particle **か** can be used with sentences containing demonstratives.

The demonstratives and other particles retain their familiar functions.

The basic polite question structure does not change.

## 12. Important Concepts

- Japanese has a systematic family of demonstrative expressions called the **こ-そ-あ-ど** system.
- **これ** refers to something near the speaker.
- **それ** refers to something near the listener.
- **あれ** refers to something away from both the speaker and listener.
- Physical distance provides the basic distinction, but context can also influence reference.
- **これ**, **それ** and **あれ** function as noun-like expressions.
- These demonstratives can stand independently without a following noun.
- Demonstratives can combine with familiar particles such as **は**, **が**, **も** and **を**.
- The grammatical functions of familiar particles remain unchanged.
- Demonstratives can be used with **です** to form noun sentences.
- They can also appear in polite questions ending with **か**.
- A complete noun phrase should be analyzed together with its modifiers.
- **これ** and **この** have different grammatical functions.
- **これ** stands independently, while **この** modifies a following noun.
- **どれ** and the **この-その-あの** group will be studied separately.
