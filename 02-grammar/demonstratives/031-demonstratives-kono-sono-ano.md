# Lesson 031 - Demonstratives: この, その, あの

## 1. What I learned

- I learned how **この**, **その** and **あの** modify nouns.
- I understand their relationship to the speaker and listener.
- I can distinguish **これ・それ・あれ** from **この・その・あの**.
- I understand why the の-group must be followed by a noun.
- I can identify complete noun phrases containing demonstratives.
- I can use these noun phrases with familiar particles such as **は**, **が**, **も** and **を**.
- I can form affirmative, negative and question sentences with demonstratives.
- I can correct common demonstrative usage errors.

## 2. What Are この, その and あの?

Japanese has a systematic family of demonstrative expressions based on the **こ・そ・あ・ど** pattern.

Lesson 030 introduced **これ**, **それ** and **あれ**, which can refer to things independently.

This lesson introduces the related forms that modify nouns.

| Demonstrative | Meaning             | Basic reference   |
| ------------- | ------------------- | ----------------- |
| この          | this ...            | Near the speaker  |
| その          | that ...            | Near the listener |
| あの          | that ... over there | Away from both    |

These demonstratives appear directly before a noun.

Basic pattern:

**Demonstrative + Noun**

<u>Example:</u>

このほん = this book\
そのペン = that pen\
あのねこ = that cat over there

The demonstrative identifies which noun is being referred to.

The noun identifies the kind of person or thing.

Unlike これ, それ and あれ, the forms studied here cannot normally stand independently.

## 3. Cure Dolly's Explanation

Cure Dolly discusses the **こ・そ・あ・ど** system in Chapter 20, "Directionals: それ・その・そんな・そう etc."

She distinguishes the **れ-group** from the **の-group**.

The れ-group includes:

- これ
- それ
- あれ
- どれ

These expressions refer independently to things or entities.

The の-group includes:

- この
- その
- あの
- どの

Cure Dolly connects the の-group to the familiar role of **の** in forming descriptions and relationships between nouns.

<u>Example:</u>

わたしのほん = my book\
このほん = this book

Both expressions contain information that modifies or identifies ほん.

However, they express different relationships.
**わたしの** relates the book to the speaker.
**この** identifies the book through its relationship to the speaker or conversational context.

The important structural idea is that **この**, **その** and **あの** modify a following noun rather than independently identifying a thing.

Tae Kim also discusses these forms in Chapter 3, Section 3.11.4, "The の particle."

He explains their connection to the の particle by describing them as shortened forms of expressions such as これの and それの.

For practical usage, treat **この・その・あの** as established demonstrative forms.

They should not simply be replaced with **これの・それの・あれの**.

## 4. The Difference Between the れ-Group and the の-Group

Compare:

| Independent form       | Noun-modifying form             |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| これ = this            | このほん = this book            |
| それ = that            | そのほん = that book            |
| あれ = that over there | あのほん = that book over there |

The important distinction is grammatical.
The れ-group functions independently as noun-like expressions.
The の-group directly modifies nouns.

<u>Example:</u>

これはほんです。= This is a book.

Here, これ refers independently to an object.

<u>Example:</u>

このほんはわたしのほんです。= This book is my book.

Here, この modifies ほん.

The complete noun phrase is **このほん**.

The two forms cannot be used interchangeably.

Incorrect:

これほん

Correct:

このほん

Incorrect:

このはほんです。

Correct:

これはほんです。

The first incorrect expression attempts to place これ directly before a noun.

The second attempts to use この independently without a following noun.

## 5. Demonstratives and Complete Noun Phrases

The combination of a demonstrative and a noun forms a noun phrase.

<u>Example:</u>

このほん = this book

そのペン = that pen

あのねこ = that cat over there

When a particle follows such a phrase, it applies to the complete noun phrase.

<u>Example:</u>

このほんはわたしのほんです。= This book is my book.

Analyze the sentence as:

［このほん］は［わたしのほんです］。

| Component         | Expression       |
| ----------------- | ---------------- |
| Topic noun phrase | このほん         |
| Topic particle    | は               |
| Predicate         | わたしのほんです |

The demonstrative **この** modifies **ほん**.

The complete topic noun phrase is **このほん**.

The particle **は** marks that complete phrase as the topic.

The predicate is **わたしのほんです**.

Do not separate the demonstrative from the noun when identifying the complete topic phrase.

## 6. Demonstratives with Familiar Particles

Noun phrases containing demonstratives can be used with familiar particles.

Compare:

| Expression | Function                        |
| ---------- | ------------------------------- |
| このほんは | Presents this book as the topic |
| そのほんが | Marks that book as the subject  |
| あのねこも | Adds that cat as well           |
| このほんを | Marks this book as the object   |

### Demonstratives with は

<u>Example:</u>

そのペンはせんせいのペンです。= That pen is the teacher's pen.

The complete topic noun phrase is **そのペン**.

The particle **は** presents it as the topic.

### Demonstratives with が

<u>Example:</u>

このほんがわたしのほんです。= This book is my book.

The particle **が** marks the complete noun phrase **このほん** as the grammatical subject.

It can be used when identifying which book is the speaker's book.

### Demonstratives with も

<u>Example:</u>

あのねこもせんせいのねこです。= That cat over there is also the teacher's cat.

The particle **も** adds the entire noun phrase **あのねこ**.

It does not add only the noun ねこ independently of its modifier.

### Demonstratives with を

<u>Example:</u>

このほんをよみます。= I read this book / I will read this book.

The complete object noun phrase is **このほん**.

The particle **を** marks that complete phrase as the direct object of よみます.

The familiar particles keep their grammatical functions.

## 7. Demonstratives and の

A noun phrase containing a demonstrative can appear together with other noun phrases connected by **の**.

<u>Example:</u>

このほんはわたしのともだちのほんです。=
This book is my friend's book.

Analyze the sentence as:

［このほん］は［わたしのともだちのほんです］。

| Component         | Expression                 |
| ----------------- | -------------------------- |
| Topic noun phrase | このほん                   |
| Predicate         | わたしのともだちのほんです |

The topic noun phrase is **このほん**.

Its head noun is ほん.

The predicate contains another noun phrase:

**わたしのともだちのほん**

Analyze the relationships:

わたしのともだち = my friend

わたしのともだちのほん = my friend's book

The first の connects わたし and ともだち.

The second の connects the complete phrase わたしのともだち with ほん.

The complete predicate includes the noun phrase and **です**.

The demonstrative **この** belongs to the topic phrase and does not change the relationships inside the predicate.

## 8. Demonstratives in Negative Noun Sentences

The の-group can be used with the negative noun-sentence pattern studied in Lesson 028.

Basic pattern:

**AはBではありません。**

<u>Example:</u>

このペンはせんせいのペンではありません。=
This pen is not the teacher's pen.

Analyze the sentence as:

［このペン］は［せんせいのペンではありません］。

| Component         | Expression                   |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Topic noun phrase | このペン                     |
| Predicate         | せんせいのペンではありません |

The topic noun phrase is **このペン**.

The negative predicate is **せんせいのペンではありません**.

The negative ending **ではありません** belongs to the predicate.

The demonstrative does not change the negative sentence pattern.

## 9. Demonstratives in Polite Questions

The の-group can also be used with the question patterns studied in Lesson 029.

<u>Example:</u>

そのほんもわたしのともだちのほんですか。=
Is that book also my friend's book?

Analyze the sentence as:

［そのほん］も［わたしのともだちのほんです］か。

| Component                         | Expression                 |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Noun phrase with additive meaning | そのほんも                 |
| Predicate                         | わたしのともだちのほんです |
| Question particle                 | か                         |

The demonstrative **その** modifies **ほん**.
The particle **も** adds the complete noun phrase そのほん.
The particle **か** marks the polite question.

<u>Example:</u>

あのねこはがっこうにいますか。= Is that cat over there at school?

| Part       | Role                       |
| ---------- | -------------------------- |
| あのねこは | topic: that cat over there |
| がっこうに | location: at school        |
| います     | predicate: is present      |
| か         | question particle          |

The complete topic noun phrase is **あのねこ**.
The particle **に** marks the location of the cat.
The question particle **か** follows the predicate.

## 10. Demonstratives with Verb Sentences

Demonstratives can be used in verb sentences without changing the familiar grammatical structure.

<u>Example:</u>

せんせいはこのほんをよみますか。= Does the teacher read this book?

Analyze the sentence as:

［せんせい］は［このほん］を［よみます］か。

| Part       | Role               |
| ---------- | ------------------ |
| せんせいは | topic: the teacher |
| このほんを | object: this book  |
| よみます   | predicate: read    |
| か         | question particle  |

The demonstrative **この** modifies the noun ほん.
The object particle **を** applies to the complete noun phrase このほん.

The familiar non-past verb form can describe habitual or future actions, depending on context.

Compare:

せんせいはこのほんをよみますか。= Does the teacher read this book?

せんせいがこのほんをよみますか。= Does the teacher read this book?

Both sentences are grammatically possible.

In the first sentence, **は** presents the teacher as the topic.
In the second sentence, **が** explicitly marks the teacher as the subject.
The choice depends on the context and information being presented.
The demonstrative noun phrase **このほん** remains the object in both sentences.

## 11. Important Distinctions

### Independent reference vs. noun modification

Compare:

これをよみます。= I read this.\
このほんをよみます。= I read this book.

In the first sentence, **これ** independently refers to an object.
In the second sentence, **この** modifies ほん.
The complete object noun phrase is **このほん**.
Both expressions can be followed by the object particle を, but their internal structures are different.

### The complete noun phrase

A demonstrative and its following noun form one noun phrase.

<u>Example:</u>

- このほん
- そのペン
- あのねこ

When identifying the topic, subject or object, include the entire noun phrase.

### Demonstratives and context

The basic physical meanings are:

- この: near the speaker
- その: near the listener
- あの: away from both

However, these forms can also refer to things established through conversation.
Their interpretation depends on context.
The physical-distance distinction is the foundation practiced in this lesson.

## 12. Important Concepts

- **この**, **その** and **あの** belong to the の-group of demonstratives.
- They directly modify following nouns.
- **この** identifies something associated with the speaker.
- **その** identifies something associated with the listener.
- **あの** identifies something away from both in the basic physical model.
- The の-group differs grammatically from the れ-group.
- **これ・それ・あれ** can stand independently.
- **この・その・あの** require a following noun.
- A demonstrative and its following noun form a complete noun phrase.
- Familiar particles apply to the complete noun phrase.
- Demonstrative noun phrases can be used with familiar affirmative, negative and question patterns.
- **これ** and **この** are not interchangeable.
- The related word **どの** and other demonstrative groups will be studied separately.
