# Lesson 027 - Noun Sentences with です

## 1. What I learned

- I learned how to make basic affirmative noun sentences with `です`.
- I can identify the topic and the complete predicate.
- I can use complete noun phrases in both parts of the sentence.
- I can combine noun sentences with `の`, `も` and `と`.
- I understand that `です` does not change according to person or number.
- I can understand an omitted topic or subject from context.
- I can distinguish identification from existence and actions.

## 2. What is a Noun Sentence?

In the pattern studied here, a noun sentence identifies or classifies someone or
something, or states a relationship.

Basic pattern:

`AはBです。`

A is the topic. B is a noun or noun phrase. The complete predicate is Bです.

Example:

わたしはがくせいです。= I am a student.

- Topic noun phrase: わたし
- Topic marker: は
- Complete predicate: がくせいです

## 3. Cure Dolly's Explanation

Cure Dolly introduces copula sentences in Chapter 1, "The Basic Types of Sentences",
in the section "Copula sentences".

Chapter 17, "Polite Japanese and the volitional", discusses `です` in the section "です".

For the noun-predicate uses studied here, `です` is the polite counterpart of `だ`.

The structural point is that a noun or noun phrase can form a predicate with the copula.
This lesson practices the polite form `です`.

This comparison is limited to the noun sentences studied here.
Uses with adjectives will be studied separately.

## 4. Basic Noun + です

Example:

わたしはがくせいです。= I am a student.\
ともだちはせんせいです。= My friend is a teacher.

The noun before `です` supplies the identifying or classifying information.

| Part         | Role                    |
| ------------ | ----------------------- |
| わたしは     | topic: I                |
| がくせいです | predicate: am a student |

は marks the topic. がくせいです is the complete predicate.

## 5. Complete Noun Phrases

Example:

わたしのともだちはがっこうのせんせいです。= My friend is a teacher at the school.

| Component                | Complete expression    | Head noun |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- | --------- |
| Topic noun phrase        | わたしのともだち       | ともだち  |
| Noun phrase in predicate | がっこうのせんせい     | せんせい  |
| Complete predicate       | がっこうのせんせいです | —         |

The sentence gives information about my friend.

わたし is a modifier inside the topic noun phrase.
せんせい is the head noun of the predicate's noun phrase.

When identifying the complete topic or predicate, include its modifiers.

## 6. Person and Number

Compare:

| Japanese                                 | Meaning                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| わたしはがくせいです。                   | I am a student.               |
| ともだちはがくせいです。                 | My friend is a student.       |
| わたしとわたしのともだちはがくせいです。 | My friend and I are students. |

`です` has the same form in all three examples.

In these patterns, it does not change according to person or number.
English uses "am", "is" and "are"; Japanese uses `です` here.

## 7. も with Noun Sentences

Example:

ともだちはがくせいです。= My friend is a student.\
わたしもがくせいです。= I am a student too.

`も` adds わたし as another person to whom the statement applies.
In this basic pattern, it replaces `は`.

Example:

せんせいのともだちもがくせいです。= The teacher's friend is a student too.

The complete phrase followed by `も` is せんせいのともだち.
The person being added is the teacher's friend.

## 8. と with Noun Sentences

Example:

わたしとわたしのともだちはがっこうのせんせいです。= My friend and I are teachers at the school.

The complete topic noun phrase is:

［わたし］と［わたしのともだち］

`と` joins the two noun phrases.
`は` marks the complete coordinated group as the topic.

The complete predicate is がっこうのせんせいです.

## 9. Omission and Context

Example:

がくせいです。= I am a student.

Here, the speaker is introducing themself.
The person being described is understood from context.

The Japanese sentence does not explicitly contain わたし.
"I" is supplied by the context.

In another context, the same Japanese sentence could describe someone else.

## 10. Sentence Analysis

Example:

がっこうのせんせいはわたしのともだちです。=
The teacher at the school is my friend.

| Part                 | Role                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------- |
| がっこうのせんせいは | topic: the school teacher |
| わたしのともだちです | predicate: is my friend   |

The topic noun phrase is がっこうのせんせい.
Its head noun is せんせい.

The noun phrase inside the predicate is わたしのともだち.
Its head noun is ともだち.

The complete predicate includes that noun phrase and `です`.

## 11. Important Distinctions

### Identification, existence and actions

| Sentence                 | Information                    |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| わたしはがくせいです。   | identification: I am a student |
| わたしはへやにいます。   | existence: I am in the room    |
| わたしはほんをよみます。 | action: I read a book          |

Use `います` for the familiar pattern describing a person's presence
in a place.

Use `noun + です` for the identification patterns studied here.

### Familiar polite verb forms

For the intended meaning "I read a book":

- Incorrect: わたしはほんをよみますです。
- Correct: わたしはほんをよみます。

Do not add `です` to the familiar affirmative verb forms ending in `ます`.

## 12. Important Concepts

- In the pattern `AはBです`, A is the topic and B is a noun or noun phrase.
- The complete predicate is B です.
- Include modifiers when identifying the complete topic or predicate.
- A head noun and a complete noun phrase are different levels of analysis.
- `です` makes these noun sentences polite.
- `です` does not change according to person or number.
- `の` expresses relationships inside noun phrases.
- `も` can add a complete noun phrase.
- `と` can join noun phrases into a coordinated topic.
- Omitted information is understood from context.
- This lesson covers basic affirmative non-past noun sentences.
- Negative forms, questions, past forms and adjective patterns will be studied separately.
