# Lesson 046 — Plain Noun Sentence Forms (だ・じゃない・だった・じゃなかった)

## 1. Plain Noun Predicates

Plain forms express noun predicates without the usual polite endings.

- だ = plain non-past affirmative
- じゃない = plain non-past negative
- だった = plain past affirmative
- じゃなかった = plain past negative

**Examples:**

この人は先生だ。= This person is a teacher.

この人は先生じゃなかった。= This person was not a teacher.

## 2. Four Basic Plain Noun Forms

|          | Affirmative | Negative         |
| -------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| Non-past | 先生だ      | 先生じゃない     |
| Past     | 先生だった  | 先生じゃなかった |

Compare the corresponding polite forms:

| Meaning              | Polite                   | Plain            |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------- |
| Non-past affirmative | 先生です                 | 先生だ           |
| Non-past negative    | 先生ではありません       | 先生じゃない     |
| Past affirmative     | 先生でした               | 先生だった       |
| Past negative        | 先生ではありませんでした | 先生じゃなかった |

Changing politeness does not change the tense or polarity.

## 3. Plain Questions and Omitted だ

In ordinary conversation, plain noun questions often use rising intonation without sentence-final か.

この人は先生？ = Is this person a teacher?

あの人は先生だった？ = Was that person a teacher?

この人は先生じゃなかった？ = Wasn't this person a teacher?

In affirmative non-past statements, だ can sometimes be omitted.

この人は日本人だ。 = This person is Japanese.

この人は日本人。 = This person is Japanese.

The choice depends on context and tone. だ may sound more assertive.

## 4. Negative Variants and Register

- じゃない = conversational plain negative
- ではない = plain negative, often more formal or written
- じゃなかった = conversational plain past negative
- ではなかった = plain past negative, often more formal or written

Conversational polite negative:

先生じゃないです。 = Is not a teacher.

The negative meaning comes from ない; です adds politeness.

Do not combine だ with these negative endings.

## 5. Noun vs. Verb Plain Forms

Noun predicates and verbs have different plain-form patterns.

| Predicate Type | Polite Non-past | Plain Non-past |
| -------------- | --------------- | -------------- |
| Noun           | 先生です        | 先生だ         |
| Verb           | 読みます        | 読む           |

A dictionary-form verb already functions as a plain non-past predicate.

Do not add だ after a dictionary-form verb.

## 6. Example Sentences

この日本人は先生じゃなかった。= This Japanese person was not a teacher.

どの日本人が先生の友達じゃなかった？ = Which Japanese person was not the teacher's friend?

あの人は学校の先生だったけど、今は先生じゃない。= That person was a teacher at the school, but is not a teacher now.

## 7. Important Concepts

- Plain noun forms distinguish tense and polarity.
- だ and だった are affirmative; じゃない and じゃなかった are negative.
- Plain questions can use intonation without か.
- Affirmative non-past だ may be omitted in some contexts.
- じゃないです is conversationally polite, not fully plain.
- Plain noun predicates and plain verb predicates follow different patterns.
