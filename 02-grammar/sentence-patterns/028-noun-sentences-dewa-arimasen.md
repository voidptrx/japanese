# Lesson 028 - Negative Noun Sentences with ではありません

## 1. What I learned

- I learned how to make basic negative noun sentences with **ではありません**.
- I can change the affirmative noun pattern into its polite negative form.
- I can identify the topic, the noun phrase in the predicate and the complete predicate.
- I can keep modifiers inside complete noun phrases.
- I can combine negative noun sentences with **の**, **も** and **と**.
- I understand that the negative form does not change according to person or number.
- I can understand omitted information from context.

## 2. What is a Negative Noun Sentence?

In the pattern studied here, a negative noun sentence denies an identification, classification or relationship.

Basic pattern:

**AはBではありません。**

A is the topic. B is a noun or noun phrase.
The complete predicate is B ではありません.

<u>Example:</u>

わたしはがくせいではありません。= I am not a student.

The sentence denies that the speaker is a student.

## 4. From Affirmative to Negative

Compare:

| Affirmative          | Negative                       |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| がくせいです         | がくせいではありません         |
| せんせいです         | せんせいではありません         |
| わたしのともだちです | わたしのともだちではありません |

For the noun sentences studied here, replace **です** with **ではありません**.

<u>Example:</u>

わたしはがくせいです。= I am a student.\
わたしはがくせいではありません。= I am not a student.

The topic and the noun remain the same.
The predicate changes from affirmative to negative.

**ではありません** is polite. The **は** inside this ending is pronounced **わ**.

## 5. Complete Noun Phrases and Predicates

<u>Example:</u>

わたしのともだちはがっこうのせんせいではありません。=
My friend is not a teacher at the school.

Analyze the sentence as:

［わたしのともだち］は［がっこうのせんせいではありません］。

| Component                | Complete expression              |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Topic noun phrase        | わたしのともだち                 |
| Noun phrase in predicate | がっこうのせんせい               |
| Complete predicate       | がっこうのせんせいではありません |

The head noun of the topic phrase is ともだち.
The head noun of the predicate's noun phrase is せんせい.

Include the modifiers when identifying the noun phrase.
Include **ではありません** when identifying the complete predicate.

The brackets are analysis aids. They are not required Japanese punctuation.

## 6. Person and Number

Compare:

| Japanese                                           | Meaning                           |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| わたしはがくせいではありません。                   | I am not a student.               |
| ともだちはがくせいではありません。                 | My friend is not a student.       |
| わたしとわたしのともだちはがくせいではありません。 | My friend and I are not students. |

`ではありません` has the same form in all three examples.

In these patterns, it does not change according to person or number.

## 7. も with Negative Noun Sentences

<u>Example:</u>

ともだちはがくせいではありません。= My friend is not a student.\
わたしもがくせいではありません。= I am not a student either.

**も** adds わたし as another person to whom the negative statement applies.
In this basic pattern, it replaces **は**.

English often uses "either" with this negative meaning.

<u>Example:</u>

せんせいのともだちもがくせいではありません。=
The teacher's friend is not a student either.

The complete phrase followed by **も** is せんせいのともだち.
Its head noun is ともだち.

## 8. と with Negative Noun Sentences

<u>Example:</u>

わたしとわたしのともだちはがっこうのせんせいではありません。=
My friend and I are not teachers at the school.

The complete topic noun phrase is:

［わたし］と［わたしのともだち］

**と** joins the two noun phrases.
**は** marks the complete coordinated group as the topic.

The complete predicate is がっこうのせんせいではありません.

## 9. Omission and Context

<u>Example:</u>

がくせいではありません。= I am not a student.

Here, the speaker is introducing themself.
The person being described is understood from context.

The Japanese sentence does not explicitly contain わたし.
"I" is supplied by the context.

In another context, the same Japanese sentence could describe someone else.

## 10. Sentence Analysis

<u>Example:</u>

わたしのせんせいはわたしのともだちのせんせいではありません。=
My teacher is not my friend's teacher.

| Part                                     | Role                                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| わたしのせんせいは                       | topic: my teacher                     |
| わたしのともだちのせんせいではありません | predicate: is not my friend's teacher |

The topic noun phrase is わたしのせんせい.

The noun phrase inside the predicate is わたしのともだちのせんせい.
Analyze it as:

［わたしのともだち］のせんせい

Its head noun is せんせい.

The complete predicate includes the whole noun phrase and `ではありません`.

## 11. Important Distinctions

### A noun phrase and the complete predicate

- Noun phrase: わたしのともだち
- Complete negative predicate: わたしのともだちではありません

The negative ending is part of the predicate.

### Spelling the negative pattern

The pattern practiced in this lesson is **ではありません**.

It contains **は** after **で**, and its final part is **ません**.

- Incorrect spelling: ではありますん
- Correct spelling: ではありません

Keep the noun or noun phrase before the negative ending:

わたしはがくせいではありません。= I am not a student.

## 12. Important Concepts

- The basic pattern is **AはBではありません**.
- B is a noun or complete noun phrase.
- The complete predicate includes B and **ではありません**.
- Keep modifiers when identifying the complete topic or noun phrase.
- **ではありません** is the polite negative form practiced in this lesson.
- It does not change according to person or number.
- **の** keeps its role inside noun phrases.
- **も** can add a complete noun phrase to a negative statement.
- **と** can join noun phrases into a coordinated topic.
- Omitted information is understood from context.
- This lesson covers basic negative non-past noun sentences.
- Questions, past forms and negative verb forms will be studied separately.
