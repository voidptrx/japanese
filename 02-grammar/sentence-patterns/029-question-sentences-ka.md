# Lesson 029 - Question Sentences with か

## 1. What I learned

- I learned how sentence-final **か** marks a polite question.
- I can form questions with noun predicates ending in **です**.
- I can form questions with familiar polite verb forms.
- I can distinguish the predicate from the question particle.
- I understand that the familiar particles keep their roles in these questions.
- I can answer basic affirmative questions with **はい** and **いいえ**.
- I can understand omitted information from context.
- I can preserve the additive meaning of **も** and **でも** in questions.

## 2. What is the Particle か?

In the use studied here, sentence-final **か** turns a polite statement
into a yes/no question.

Basic patterns:

**AはBですか。**\
Polite verb form + か。

<u>Example:</u>

せんせいのともだちはがくせいです。= The teacher's friend is a student.\
せんせいのともだちはがくせいですか。= Is the teacher's friend a student?

Keep the word order and add **か** at the end.

## 4. Questions with Noun Predicates

<u>Example:</u>

がっこうのせんせいはわたしのともだちですか。= Is the teacher at the school my friend?

Analyze the sentence as:

［がっこうのせんせい］は［わたしのともだちです］か。

| Component         | Expression           |
| ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Topic noun phrase | がっこうのせんせい   |
| Predicate         | わたしのともだちです |
| Question particle | か                   |

The noun phrase inside the predicate is わたしのともだち.

The predicate includes that noun phrase and **です**.
The question particle follows the completed predicate.

The brackets are analysis aids. They are not required Japanese punctuation.

## 5. Questions with Familiar Verb Forms

Compare:

| Familiar polite form | Question form |
| -------------------- | ------------- |
| よみます             | よみますか    |
| いきます             | いきますか    |
| います               | いますか      |

<u>Example:</u>

せんせいはほんをよみますか。= Does the teacher read books?

<u>Example:</u>

ねこはへやにいますか。= Is the cat in the room?

Keep the familiar polite verb form and place **か** after it.

## 6. Answering with はい and いいえ

New words:

| Kana   | Meaning |
| ------ | ------- |
| はい   | yes     |
| いいえ | no      |

Context: someone asks whether you are a student.

がくせいですか。= Are you a student?

Affirmative answer:

はい、がくせいです。= Yes, I am a student.

Negative answer:

いいえ、がくせいではありません。= No, I am not a student.

These answers are statements.
They do not take the question particle **か**.

## 7. Omission and Context

In the previous exchange, the Japanese question does not explicitly
name the person being asked about:

がくせいですか。= Are you a student?

The listener understands that the speaker is asking about them.

In the answer, the speaker can also omit わたし:

はい、がくせいです。= Yes, I am a student.

The context identifies the person in each sentence.
**か** marks the question; it does not itself specify a person.

## 8. Other Particles Keep Their Roles

<u>Example:</u>

せんせいはわたしのともだちとバスでがっこうにいきますか。=
Does the teacher go to school by bus with my friend?

| Part               | Role                      |
| ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| せんせいは         | topic: the teacher        |
| わたしのともだちと | companion: with my friend |
| バスで             | transportation: by bus    |
| がっこうに         | destination: to school    |
| いきます           | predicate: go             |
| か                 | question particle         |

**の** expresses a relationship inside わたしのともだち.
The other particles retain their familiar roles in this question.

## 9. Sentence Analysis

<u>Example:</u>

Context: the speakers are discussing tomorrow's plan.

せんせいははちじからくじまでへやでほんをよみますか。=
Will the teacher read a book in the room from eight to nine o'clock?

| Part       | Role                           |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ |
| せんせいは | topic: the teacher             |
| はちじから | starting time: from eight      |
| くじまで   | ending time: until nine        |
| へやで     | location of the reading action |
| ほんを     | object: a book                 |
| よみます   | predicate: read                |
| か         | question particle              |

The future interpretation comes from the plan context.

The familiar non-past verb forms can also describe habitual actions.
The context determines the interpretation of the question.

## 10. Important Distinctions

### Question marking and punctuation

The examples in this lesson end in **か。**.

**か** is the grammatical question marker.
**。** is the punctuation mark ending the sentence.

## 11. Important Concepts

- Sentence-final **か** can mark a polite yes/no question.
- The basic noun pattern is A は B ですか.
- Add **か** after the familiar polite verb form.
- Preserve the word order when forming these questions.
- The question particle follows the completed predicate.
- The familiar particles keep their roles in these basic examples.
- **はい** and **いいえ** can introduce answers to the affirmative questions studied here.
- Use a statement when giving the answers practiced in this lesson.
- Omitted information is understood from context.
- Context distinguishes habitual actions from future plans.
- This lesson practices questions formed from affirmative polite clauses and familiar noun-sentence answers.
- Negative questions, question-word patterns and other uses of **か** will be studied separately.
