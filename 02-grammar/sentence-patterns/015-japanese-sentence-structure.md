# Lesson 015 - Japanese Sentence Structure

## 1. What I learned

- I learned that the predicate normally appears at the end of a Japanese sentence.
- I learned that Japanese predicates can contain verbs, nouns or adjectives.
- I understand the basic role of particles in Japanese sentences.
- I learned that `は` marks the topic, not necessarily the grammatical subject.
- I learned that Japanese often omits information that is clear from context.
- I understand some similarities between Japanese and Turkish word order.

## 2. The Predicate

The predicate is the central element of a Japanese sentence.

In Japanese, the predicate normally appears at the end.

Examples:

- みずをのみます。= Drink water.
- わたしはがくせいです。= I am a student.
- ねこはかわいいです。= The cat is cute.

Japanese sentences can have different predicate types.

| Type      | Example                | Predicate    |
| --------- | ---------------------- | ------------ |
| Verb      | ほんをよみます。       | よみます     |
| Noun      | わたしはがくせいです。 | がくせいです |
| Adjective | きょうはさむいです。   | さむいです   |

A predicate does not always have to be a verb.

## 3. Basic Word Order

Japanese and Turkish both generally place the predicate at the end of a sentence.

Example:

わたしはみずをのみます。

- わたしは = topic
- みずを = object
- のみます = predicate

A common beginner sentence pattern is:

Topic + Object + Predicate

However, this is not a fixed rule for every Japanese sentence.

Japanese word order allows some flexibility.
Particles help identify the roles of different expressions.

## 4. Particles

Particles usually follow the words or phrases they mark.

Two particles introduced in this lesson:

### は - Topic Marker

The particle `は` marks the topic of a sentence.

Example:

わたしはがくせいです。

- わたし = I
- は = topic marker
- がくせいです = am a student

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - The topic particle is written `は`.
> - As a topic particle, `は` is pronounced `wa`.
> - It is not written わ.
> - A topic is not necessarily the grammatical subject.

### を - Object Marker

The particle `を` marks the direct object.

Example:

ほんをよみます。

- ほん = book
- を = object marker
- よみます = read

The particle `を` is normally pronounced `o`.

## 5. Topic vs. Subject

The topic tells us what the sentence is about.

The grammatical subject and the topic are not always the same.

Example:

ねこはかわいいです。

Here, ねこ is the topic and can also be understood as the subject.

Compare:

きょうはさむいです。

Here, きょう (today) is the topic and a time expression.

The particle は does not automatically identify the grammatical subject.

## 6. Omitted Information

Japanese often leaves out information that is understood from context.

Compare:

わたしはみずをのみます。= I drink water.\
みずをのみます。= Drink water / will drink water.

In the second sentence, the person performing the action is not explicitly identified.
The context determines who is drinking.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> - Do not automatically assume the subject is "I".
> - Do not assume every word in an English or Turkish translation is explicitly present in the Japanese sentence.
> - Distinguish expressed information from contextual information.

## 7. Important Concepts

- The predicate normally comes last.
- A predicate can be verbal, nominal or adjectival.
- Particles help identify grammatical relationships.
- `は` marks the topic and is pronounced `wa`.
- `を` marks the direct object.
- Topic and subject are different concepts.
- Japanese frequently omits contextually understood information.
- Japanese non-past verb forms may refer to present, habitual or future situations depending on context.
- Understanding sentence structure is more important than translating word by word.
