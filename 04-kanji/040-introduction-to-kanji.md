# Lesson 040 — Introduction to Kanji (漢字)

## 1. What Is Kanji?

Kanji (漢字) are Chinese-origin characters used in Japanese to write words and meaningful word components.

Unlike kana, a kanji can have multiple readings depending on the word.

Japanese uses three writing systems:

- Hiragana: phonetic script.
- Katakana: phonetic script, often used for loanwords.
- Kanji: characters associated with meanings and readings.

## 2. Kanji Readings

### On'yomi (音読み)

Readings historically derived from Chinese pronunciations.

### Kun'yomi (訓読み)

Readings associated with native Japanese words.

**Example:** 人 (person)

- 人（ひと）— Kun'yomi
- 日本人（にほんじん）— じん is On'yomi

Single kanji words often use Kun'yomi, while multi-kanji compounds often use On'yomi. This is a tendency, not an absolute rule.

Learn readings through vocabulary rather than memorizing every possible reading in isolation.

## 3. First Five Kanji

| Kanji | Core Meaning    | Example                  |
| ----- | --------------- | ------------------------ |
| 日    | Sun, day        | 日本（にほん）— Japan    |
| 本    | Book, origin    | 本（ほん）— Book         |
| 人    | Person          | 人（ひと）— Person       |
| 学    | Learning, study | 学校（がっこう）— School |
| 校    | School          | 学校（がっこう）— School |

### Compound Words

- 日本（にほん）— Japan
- 日本人（にほんじん）— Japanese person
- 学校（がっこう）— School

Compound readings can undergo sound changes.

For example, 学校 is pronounced がっこう, not がくこう. The small っ represents consonant gemination.

## 4. Stroke Order (筆順)

Kanji are written using established stroke orders.

Common principles:

- Generally top to bottom.
- Generally left to right.
- Character-specific rules and exceptions apply.

**Example**:

- 人 — 2 strokes
- 日 — 4 strokes
- 本 — 5 strokes

Practice correct order, direction, and proportions.

## 5. Okurigana (送り仮名)

Okurigana are kana written after kanji, commonly used with verbs and adjectives.

**Example:**

読む（よむ）— To read
読みます（よみます）— Polite non-past form

読 is the kanji; the following kana express the rest of the word and its inflection.

## 6. Learning Strategy

1. Learn kanji through meaningful vocabulary.
2. Associate each word with its pronunciation and meaning.
3. Recognize familiar characters in new compounds.
4. Practice stroke order and handwriting.
5. Review regularly using spaced repetition.

**Core principle:** Learn characters in the context of real Japanese words and sentences.
