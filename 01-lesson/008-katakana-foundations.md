## 2. What is Katakana?

Katakana is one of the two Japanese kana writing systems.\
Hiragana and katakana represent the same basic sounds.

Examples:

- あ = ア = a
- き = キ = ki
- し = シ = shi

Katakana is commonly used for:

- Foreign loanwords
- Foreign names
- Animal and plant names in certain contexts
- Onomatopoeia and mimetic expressions
- Emphasis and stylistic effects

## 4. Important Character Distinctions

### シ and ツ

- シ = shi
- ツ = tsu

The direction and arrangement of the strokes differ.

### ソ and ン

- ソ = so
- ン = n

These characters look similar but represent different sounds.

### ロ, コ, and ク

- ロ = ro
- コ = ko
- ク = ku

Visual differences:

- ロ has a closed rectangular shape.
- コ is open on the left.
- ク has an angular shape.

### Other Distinctions

- ヨ = yo
- エ = e
- ニ = ni
- ナ = na

## 6. Important Concepts

- Katakana does not introduce a new sound system.
- Basic katakana follow the same mora rules as hiragana.
- シ = shi, チ = chi, ツ = tsu, フ = fu.
- ヲ is normally pronounced o in modern Japanese.
- ン represents one nasal mora.
