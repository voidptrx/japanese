# Lesson 013 - Katakana Long Sounds (Chouon)

## 1. What I learned

- I learned how the long vowel mark `ー` works in katakana.
- I understand that `ー` extends the preceding vowel by one mora.
- I learned that vowel length can change the meaning of a Japanese word.
- I can count mora in words containing long vowels, yoon, sokuon and ン.

## 2. What are Long Vowels?

Japanese distinguishes short and long vowels.
A long vowel takes twice as much time as a short vowel.

In katakana, long vowels are commonly represented by `ー`
(chouonpu / 長音符).

The mark extends the vowel of the preceding kana.

Examples:

- カー = kaa = 2 mora
- キー = kii = 2 mora
- クー = kuu = 2 mora
- ケー = kee = 2 mora
- コー = koo = 2 mora

Each `ー` adds one mora.

### Examples in Words

- ケーキ = keeki = 3 mora
- コーヒー = koohii = 4 mora
- スーパー = suupaa = 4 mora
- ジュース = juusu = 3 mora

### Vowel Length and Meaning

- ビル = biru = 2 mora = building
- ビール = biiru = 3 mora = beer

Vowel length can change the meaning of a word.

### Long Vowels with Yoon and Sokuon

Long vowels can appear together with yoon and sokuon.

Example:

ジュース = juusu = 3 mora

- ジュ = 1 mora
- ー = 1 mora
- ス = 1 mora

Another example:

クッキー = kukkii = 4 mora

- ク = 1 mora
- ッ = 1 mora
- キ = 1 mora
- ー = 1 mora

Important distinctions:

- Small ャ / ュ / ョ combines with the previous kana.
- Small ッ adds one mora.
- ン counts as one mora.
- The long vowel mark ー adds one mora.
- The mark ー extends the preceding vowel, not the following kana.

For learning purposes, long vowels are written with
double vowels in romaji: aa, ii, uu, ee, oo.

Long vowels may also be represented with macrons,
such as kōhī instead of koohii.
