# Lesson 006 - Nasal Mora (ん)

## 1. What I learned

- I learned why `ん` is different from most other hiragana.
- I understand that `ん` counts as one mora.
- I learned that the pronunciation of `ん` can change depending on surrounding sounds.
- I learned how to distinguish `ん` + `や` from yoon combinations.
- I can read words containing `ん` and count their mora correctly.

## 2. What is the Nasal Mora?

The hiragana `ん` represents a nasal mora.

Unlike most basic hiragana, `ん` does not contain a vowel.

However, it still counts as one mora.

Examples:

- ほ = ho = 1 mora
- ほん = hon = 2 mora
- にほん = nihon = 3 mora
- しんぶん = shinbun = 4 mora

## 3. Pronunciation Changes

The written character `ん` stays the same, but its
pronunciation can change depending on the following sound.

### Before p, b, and m

`ん` often sounds similar to m.

Example:

- さんぽ = sanpo

### Before k and g

`ん` often sounds similar to the final ng sound
in the English word "sing".

Example:

- てんき = tenki

### At the End of a Word

`ん` can have a different nasal pronunciation
depending on context.

Example:

- ほん = hon

Important:

These pronunciation differences do not change
the hiragana spelling or mora count.

## 4. ん and Yoon

The following are different:

- にゃ = nya = 1 mora
- にや = niya = 2 mora
- んや = n'ya = 2 mora

A small `ゃ` combines with the preceding kana.

The character `ん` remains a separate mora.

Example:

ほんや = hon'ya = 3 mora

- ほ = 1 mora
- ん = 1 mora
- や = 1 mora

In Hepburn romaji, an apostrophe can distinguish
`ん` from a following vowel or y-sound.

## 5. Mora Counting

| Hiragana | Romaji  | Mora |
| -------- | ------- | ---- |
| ほん     | hon     | 2    |
| さんぽ   | sanpo   | 3    |
| てんき   | tenki   | 3    |
| しんぶん | shinbun | 4    |
| かんじ   | kanji   | 3    |
| にほん   | nihon   | 3    |
| ほんや   | hon'ya  | 3    |
| でんしゃ | densha  | 3    |

## 6. Important Distinctions

- Small `ゃ` / `ゅ` / `ょ` combines with the previous kana.
- Small `っ` counts as one mora.
- `ん` counts as one mora.
- The pronunciation of `ん` may change, but its spelling remains the same.
- `ん` followed by `や` is not automatically a yoon combination.
